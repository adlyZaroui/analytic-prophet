\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Prophet's MAP estimate is not the posterior mode. The Laplace prior on the
changepoint rates puts the optimum where the objective is not differentiable, and the
L-BFGS and Newton methods Prophet takes from Stan, built for smooth objectives, stop
short of it without reporting that they have. The evidence isolates the kink as the
cause. Smoothing the prior inside Prophet's own program, with nothing else changed,
brings Stan's L-BFGS between \SmoothFactorLong{} and \SmoothFactorShort{} times
closer, and one Newton code reaches the optimum on the split parameterisation and not
on the natural one. Splitting each rate into non-negative parts is an exact fix that
reaches the optimum's exact zeros, and it can be applied from outside Prophet's Stan
model, which need not change. On the held-out M4 census the corrected optimum
forecasts better on \RmseWinRate\% of the series, by a median of \RmseAdvantage\% in
RMSE: the defect is real, and its cost to forecast accuracy is usually small.
