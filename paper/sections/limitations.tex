\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The gap shrinks with series length.} The posterior margin falls
        by a factor of \LpDecline{} as a share of the posterior between $T = 300$
        and $T = 2905$, and is not monotone in absolute terms.
  \item \textbf{M4 barely exercises what Prophet is built for.} M4 is anonymised
        and its dates are synthetic, so holiday and day-of-week effects have no
        held-out evidence here at all.
  \item \textbf{Both implementations badly under-cover.} Each nominal 80\%
        interval contains about a third of the held-out points, a shared failure
        larger than anything separating the two.
  \item \textbf{Our intervals are wider.} Across the census they are wider than
        Prophet's by a median of 2.47 in the series' own units --- a loss.
  \item \textbf{The distribution has long tails both ways.} On the worst series
        for this implementation Prophet's forecast is better by 78.7\%.
  \item \textbf{Two of M4's six frequencies.} The census is every weekly and
        daily series the protocol can measure; monthly, quarterly, yearly and
        hourly are absent.
  \item \textbf{Speed at small $T$ is mostly not the gradient.} Prophet pays a
        fixed cost to launch a CmdStan subprocess, which dominates when there is
        least to fit.
  \item \textbf{Significance is uninformative at this $n$.} Every comparison
        rejects at p-values far below any conventional threshold, which
        establishes only that the direction is consistent.
\end{itemize}
