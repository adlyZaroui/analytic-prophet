\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What a Prophet user can do today.}
Prophet already ships the method that comes closest. Passing
\code{algorithm="Newton"} to \code{fit} runs Stan's Newton method at any length, and
on the Peyton Manning series it stops between \NewtonStanMin{} and \NewtonStanMax{}
nats short of the optimum (\cref{sec:evidence:newton}). We ran it on the
\NewtonCensusSeries{} census series drawn at random for \cref{sec:evidence:kkt}
(\code{paper/experiments/newton_census.py}) and measured each fit from the converged
point of that section. Prophet's default L-BFGS stops a median of
\NewtonCensusLbfgsMedian{} nats short of it, and its Newton method
\NewtonCensusNewtonMedian, closer on every one of them, at a median of
\NewtonCensusTimeRatio{} times the time a fit takes. Nor is Prophet's Newton the
optimum: it stays below it on every series, no rate comes out exactly zero, and its
relaxed residual has a median of \NewtonCensusRelaxedMedian. This implementation's
fit stops a median of $\ConvergedGainMedian$ nats short, and Prophet's Newton stops
closer than that on \NewtonCensusAbove{} of the series, by at most
\NewtonCensusAboveMax{} nats. Tighter tolerances do not rescue Prophet's L-BFGS.
With all five of its stopping tests at machine precision the gap remains
(\cref{sec:evidence:lp}), and where that makes the line search fail, Prophet's
fall-back to Newton passes the same tolerances on, cmdstanpy rejects them for Newton,
and the fit raises an error.

\paragraph{Adopting the fix with Prophet's Stan model frozen.}
Prophet's 2023 update says that ``at this moment we do not plan to make any more
changes to the underlying Stan model'' \citep{duong2023prophet}. The fix does not
need it to. A step outside Stan can start where Prophet's optimizer stops and solve
the split problem of \cref{sec:reformulation} with nothing but the frozen program's
own gradient. Stan's gradient of $\lp$ in $\delta_j$ is minus the smooth part's,
minus $\operatorname{sign}(\delta_j)/\tau$ with $\operatorname{sign}(0) = 0$
(\cref{sec:evidence:kkt}), so the smooth part's gradient is recovered exactly by
taking the prior's known term back out. We ran that step: SciPy's L-BFGS-B on the split
problem, started from Prophet's own solution, with every objective and gradient taken
from cmdstanpy's \code{log_prob} of Prophet's unmodified program
(\code{paper/experiments/polish.py}). At each of the lengths of \cref{sec:evidence:lp}
it ends at the corrected optimum or just above it --- by at most $\FrozenAboveMax$
nats, its tolerance being tighter than this implementation's default --- with as many
rates exactly zero as the optimum has, in \FrozenCallsMin{} to \FrozenCallsMax{} calls
of \code{log_prob}. Through cmdstanpy each call is a CmdStan subprocess, and the step takes
\FrozenSecondsMin{} to \FrozenSecondsMax{} seconds; BridgeStan evaluates the same
gradient in memory \citep{roualdes2023bridgestan}. The alternatives inside Stan do not
reach the optimum as they stand: neither smooth reparametrisation of
\cref{sec:related} does on Stan's own L-BFGS, and the split stated in Stan cannot
reach a zero (\cref{sec:implementation}).

\paragraph{How far the result reaches.}
Nothing in the mechanism is particular to Prophet. A Stan program fitted with
\code{optimize} whose density contains the absolute value of a parameter, through
\code{double_exponential} as Prophet's does or through an explicit \code{abs}, puts
the same kink in front of the same optimizers. How short they stop depends, as here,
on how many coordinates its optimum sets to zero (\cref{sec:evidence:surrogate}), and
no program but Prophet's is measured in this paper. Prophet's R package fits the same
program the same way. In version \EnvProphet{} of Prophet's repository its Stan file
differs from the Python package's only in blank lines, and it is optimized by L-BFGS,
by Newton below 100 observations, with the same fall-back to Newton.

\paragraph{What is out of scope.}
Everything here concerns the MAP estimate. With \code{mcmc_samples} above zero,
Prophet samples the posterior with Stan's No-U-Turn sampler instead of optimizing it,
and a posterior mean is an average of draws, which is never exactly sparse. Nothing
in this paper is a claim about sampling.
